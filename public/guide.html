The complete how-to for Background Studio.

Make backgrounds you can stand in front of.

Background Studio generates photographic background scenes to sit behind your green-screen (chroma-key) footage — the custom backdrops video creators composite themselves into. This guide walks through every feature, from your first prompt to a broadcast-ready export.

· How it works, in one breath

You describe a scene → the app generates it at the resolution and shape you choose → you can edit the result (adjust, add/replace/remove things, reframe, upscale) → review it by keying yourself over it right in the browser → then export it at an exact broadcast size and drop it behind your footage. Everything you make is saved to a local library on your own machine.

Images are generated by Nano Banana Pro (Google's Gemini 3 Pro Image) through OpenRouter, so each generation costs a small amount of credit. The header shows your remaining balance; every image shows what it cost.

1 Quick start

Your first background in under a minute:

  1. Describe the scene. Click pills under Scene & subject (and the other groups) to build a prompt, and/or just type in the prompt box. Pills and your typed text merge into one editable brief.
  2. Pick the shape and size. Set the aspect ratio (16:9 for landscape, 9:16 for Shorts/Reels, 1:1 for square) and resolution (1K / 2K / 4K).
  3. Generate. Hit ✦ Generate. A "Generating" overlay appears; when it's done, your image lands at the top of the library below.

That's the core loop. Everything else in this guide makes that loop better — richer prompts, precise edits, and a proper preview of how you'll look in front of the result.

2 Writing a prompt

Pills + free text

The guided prompt builder gives you pills for scene & subject, objects & props, style, light & time, mood & colour, camera & composition, and detail. Click any that fit. Whatever you also type in the box is merged in — in Generate mode your scene leads and your text follows; in Adjust mode your instruction leads. Use Reset pills to clear selections and start over.

Enhance prompt (optional)

Flip the Enhance prompt toggle on and the app expands your short brief into a rich, detailed scene description using a fast text model. When it's on, the expanded version appears in its own editable field so you can fine-tune it before generating — the enhanced text is what actually gets sent. Leave the toggle off to send your prompt exactly as written.

Describe an image

Have a reference photo whose look you want to reuse? Click ⌗ Describe an image in the action bar and choose a file. A multimodal model writes a reusable background-scene prompt from it straight into the prompt box — tweak it, then generate variations on that vibe. (It describes the environment, not any specific person.)

Want to see exactly what will be sent? Click ◎ Preview prompt to assemble (and, if the toggle is on, enhance) the final text without generating.

Reference images

Drop images into the Reference images area (or click to choose) to steer the look — the model uses them as visual references for the new background. In Adjust mode these same slots become the objects to add (see Editing).

3 Output settings

Aspect ratio

16:9 landscape (standard video), 9:16 tall (Shorts / Reels / TikTok), or 1:1 square. This sets the true shape of the generated frame.

Resolution — 1K / 2K / 4K

These are real quality tiers, not hints. Each step roughly doubles the pixels on every side (so ~4× the total pixels): a 16:9 image is about 1376×768 at 1K, 2752×1536 at 2K, and 5504×3072 at 4K. Hover a resolution to see the exact dimensions and megapixels for the current aspect ratio. Higher tiers look sharper but cost more and take longer.

Images (batch)

Generate 1–4 variations in one run. They're produced in parallel and all saved to the library, so you can pick the best.

Seed

Leave the seed blank for a random result each time. Set a number to make a look reproducible — the same seed + same prompt + same settings gives the same image. Every card shows the seed it used; click it to reuse it. Batches vary the seed per image so the variations differ.

Keying-safe

When Keying-safe is on, the prompt steers the scene away from pure chroma green and blue, so the finished background won't collide with your key colour and vanish when you composite. Turn it on for green-screen work; turn it off if you actually want green/blue in the scene. It's a steer, not a guarantee — glance at the result.

4 Generating

Press ✦ Generate. While it runs, a full-screen "Generating" overlay shows progress (e.g. Generating 2 / 4 for a batch). New results appear at the top of the library, newest first, each with its prompt, settings, seed, and cost.

The header balance updates right after a run. It reconciles against OpenRouter a few seconds later, so a brief settling of the number is normal.

Describe, Preview and Generate can't overlap — kick one off and the others wait, so you never fire a duplicate (billed) request by accident.

5 Editing an image

Every result can be edited without starting from scratch. Editing always produces a new image (the original is untouched) and links back to its parent.

Adjust with a new prompt

Load an image as the base in one of two ways: click a card's Adjust button, or drag the card onto the base slot at the top (a floating drop bar makes the target obvious). Type what to change, then hit Adjust. To go back to a fresh generation, clear the base slot.

Add objects

In Adjust mode, upload a product or prop photo into the reference area and describe where it goes ("place this on the desk, matching the lighting"). The model composites it into the scene.

Region editor — Add / Replace / Remove

For a targeted change, click ✂ Mark region, choose a mode, and draw a box on the image:

  • Add — put something new in the box (type what, or use an uploaded object photo).
  • Replace — swap whatever's in the box for something else.
  • Remove — erase what's in the box and fill it seamlessly with the surrounding background.

The app builds a mask and a positional hint so the edit stays confined to the region while the rest of the scene is preserved. The chip shows your active region; Edit region lets you redraw it.

Reframe / outpaint

Need the same scene in a different shape? Reframe extends (out-paints) a background into a new aspect ratio — e.g. turn a 16:9 shot into 9:16 for Shorts — continuing the scene naturally instead of cropping or regenerating.

Upscale

Upscale re-renders an image at a higher tier (2K / 4K) with enhanced detail. It works on a library image (the Upscale action) or on any image from your computer (Upscale an image in the library header) — and the result is saved to your library either way. Reach for this when you generated small and later need a bigger file, or before exporting at a size larger than the source.

Upscale is a generative re-render, not a lossless enlargement — it reinterprets fine detail, so check the result. Like all edits, it's billed as a new generation.

6 Reviewing your shot

Click any image to open it full-size. The lightbox has three review tools (one at a time):

Keying preview — see yourself in the shot

The big one. Open Keying preview, upload a green-screen frame of yourself, and the app chroma-keys it in the browser and composites it over the background — a live preview of the real composite, with no API cost. You can:

  • Pick your key colour (green or blue).
  • Tune the key threshold and edge softness until the fringe is clean.
  • Drag to position, scale, and flip yourself.
  • Save the composite as a PNG.

It's the fastest way to confirm a background actually leaves room for you before you commit to it.

Before / after compare

On an edited image, Compare shows a draggable divider between the edit and the version it came from — so you can see exactly what changed.

Subject placement guide

Drop a draggable, resizable silhouette (with a rule-of-thirds grid) onto a background to check the negative space fits your keyed subject — handy when you're planning composition before shooting.

7 Filters & grading

Open any image, click Filters, and grade it right in the browser — no AI, no cost, instant. Filters are non-destructive: the original is never changed, and everything resets when you close the image. The same render drives the live preview, the keying preview, and whatever you export, so what you see is exactly what you get.

Presets

One-tap looks: Warm, Cool, Vivid, Muted, Vintage, Sepia, B&W, Noir, and Cinema (plus None). A preset sets the colour grade; the sliders then fine-tune it.

Adjustments

  • Bright · Contrast · Saturation — the core colour tweaks.
  • Blur — a soft out-of-focus / depth-of-field look, so a keyed subject pops off a defocused background. It's scaled to the image, so the preview matches the export.
  • Vignette — darken the edges to draw the eye inward.
  • Grain — filmic texture.

Blur, vignette, and grain are independent of the colour preset, so you can combine any grade with any of them. Reset clears everything.

See it under your keyed shot

Your grade flows into the Keying preview — switch to it and you'll see your keyed subject over the graded and blurred background, i.e. the real composite. If a grade pushes the image heavy into chroma green/blue (which keys poorly), a small warning appears.

Keep the result

  • Download — save a one-off graded image at full resolution, in your chosen format (JPEG / PNG / WebP).
  • Save to library — store the graded version as a new library item (original untouched, no cost). It links back to its source, so you can compare before/after, and you can then adjust, reframe, upscale, key, or export it like any other image.

8 Exporting

Click Download on a card and choose a size:

  • Original — the file exactly as generated.
  • 1080p — 1920×1080.
  • 4K UHD — 3840×2160.

The presets export to those exact broadcast dimensions (cover-cropped to fit the target perfectly), so the file drops straight onto a 1080p or 4K timeline. A preset that's larger than the source is disabled — a ? next to it explains you'd need to Upscale first, since blowing a small image up on export would just soften it.

File format

Choose JPEG · PNG · WebP for the output: a toggle on the download menu (remembered between sessions), and a small dropdown when you Download or Save to library from the Filters panel. JPEG is the default — small and great for delivery; PNG is lossless (no added compression artifacts, but much larger files); WebP is smaller than JPEG. It applies to the 1080p / 4K UHD presets and the Filters downloads/saves; Original always gives you the untouched source file as-is.

9 The library

Everything you generate or edit is saved locally and listed newest-first. From here you can:

  • Search by prompt text.
  • Favourite ⭐ the keepers and filter to just those.
  • Sort by newest, oldest, or cost; page through results (your position persists across refreshes).
  • Read each image's metadata — aspect ratio · tier · dimensions · megapixels · cost · seed.
  • Copy the prompt that made it, Download, or Delete it.

Thumbnails are shown at a uniform size for a tidy grid; click one to see the full picture at its true dimensions.

10 Tips & good to know

Get clean keys

Turn Keying-safe on for anything you'll composite, then use the Keying preview to verify the edges before you export. Backgrounds with strong depth and clear negative space where you'll stand key best.

Reproducibility & iteration

When you land a look you like, note its seed (click it to reuse) — then change one thing at a time to iterate deliberately instead of rerolling everything.

Sizing & the "≈"

For the three measured ratios (16:9, 9:16, 1:1) the dimension labels are exact. For reframe/upload ratios (like 4:5 or 21:9) you'll see an ≈ — those sizes are close estimates. Export presets always land on exact broadcast dimensions regardless.

SynthID watermark & cost. Every generated image carries an invisible SynthID watermark marking it as AI-made — this is permanent and doesn't block commercial use. Generation and every edit are billed per image; keep credit on your OpenRouter account and watch the header balance.

11 Shortcuts & interactions

  • Ctrl/⌘ + Enter in the prompt box — Generate.
  • Drag a library card onto the base slot to adjust it; drop onto the reference area to use it as a reference.
  • In the region editor and keying preview, drag to position and the on-screen controls to scale/flip.
